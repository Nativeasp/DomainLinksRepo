6.0  
Definitions 
Abbreviations & 
Acronyms:  
 
 6.1 Approval Body  is a specific group with authority to provide formal approval.  
6.2 Business Case : is a written proposal to an authority by a unit or department 
employee seeking funding, approval or both for an activity, initiative or 
project. A Business Case  focus is the evaluation of a proposed course of action 
based on analysis of options and one tha t precedes a Project Charter . 
6.3 Close  Out Report (COR): is a documented report of project success and 
failures, lessons learned, and one that formalizes the completion of a project. 
It is the collection, organization and archiving of project documentation. A  
COR is also known as post mortem report.  
6.4 Deliverable : is any unique and verifiable product, result or capability to 
perform a service, which must be produced to complete a project and that is 
subject to approval by the project sponsor or client.  
6.5 Milestone : is a scheduled event of a project for monitoring and measurement 
of work performance. Milestones are observable and serve as a progress 
marker to  report to key leadership and project stakeholders.  
6.6 Task Assignment : is an assignment determined by the super visor for a task to 
be done that may be linked to an existing project or used to document and 
track a task not linked to a project.  
6.7 Project : is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, 
service or result.  A project require s commitment of  resources besides 
employee time and may need  unit budget allocation  or a request to Finance , 
Administration and Operations Committee  for project support funding.  
6.8 Project  Manager: is the person responsible for leading a project from its 
inception to execution  and for the project outcome and success.  Work   
includes planning, execution and managing the people, resources and scope 
of the project.   
6.9 Risk: is an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or 
negative effect on a project’ s objectives.  
6.10 SOD Designate: refers to a Strategic and Organizational Development (SOD) 
Unit  staff person  pre-assigned to a MCK unit or department  to help with 
guidance and support in the implementation of this policy.

ANNEX C Project Charter  
  
 
 
 
 
 Project Name: what is the project called? The name reflects the primary 
purpose of the project.  
Project Sponsor: a sponsor is the person or group with formal authority. 
Identify the name and position title.   
Project Manager: the manager of all aspects of the project for the charter, 
cost, human resources, risks, scope, schedule, procurement, quality  and for 
communications for the project life cycle.  
Inquiries: identify the contact information that directs user to a website, 
resources or personnel where inquiries and clarification can be made. Identify 
position title that identifies the contact.  
 PROJECT GOAL  
Is a broad statement of where  you want to go – what you are trying to achieve. A goal is an observable 
and measureable result that has one or more objectives achieved within a period. A goal is like a ladder 
that brings you to the goal with the rungs being the objectives of how to get  there.  
 
 
 
BACKGROUND  
What problem does the project solve? Were other options considered?  Provide background information 
on the problem or opportunity or business need the project is responding to  in sufficient detail to provide 
context .  
 
 
JUSTIFICATION  
This is the purpose of the project. It describes project drivers and links to benefits that the project will 
bring. This element should list in bullet form as much as possible for drivers and benefits.  
 
 
PROJECT OBJECTIVES  
Objectives are clear statements of specific results that must be achieved. Should be SMART (Specific, 
Measurable, Achievable, Realistic and Time -Oriented)as much as possible in bullet format. Include 
related success criteria.

 STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT  
Make a list of strategic goals or objectives that the project is supporting and is in alignment with. Identify 
impacts of political, community, operational or financial in nature. How does the proposed project align 
with known strategic direction or stated goals from the  MCK organization and/or from for Unit key 
leadership. What priorities from Chief and Council align with the project? Create a list of strategic goals 
or objectives that the project aligns to and supports. If selection is an MCK organizational goal(s) and/ or 
objective(s), indicate the pillar from which the goal or objective connects . 
PROJECT DELIVERABLES AND SCOPE (Tangible Results)  
A list or description of a key deliverable(s) of the project and verifiable outcomes that contributes to the 
achievement of the objective(s).  This section includes statements that describe the scope of the project – 
what the project will include to meet the requirements of the project goals or objectives and what the 
project will not include. A list of constraints (any limits like time or cost) to the project is included in this 
section.  
ASSUMPTIONS  
Describe in a statement or two what assumptions or what is held to be true for this project. Assumptions 
are unknown variables that, given time, you will find out about.  
MILESTONE SCHEDULE  
A milestone schedule indicates when key events are estimated to be achieved. These are milestones that 
are expected to take place. Include timeframe estimates. This could be a list or a table.  
 
SUMMARY BUDGET  
This indicates the project’s preliminary budget if this is identified. Include project stages that will require 
budgetary needs. Items and estimated monetary amount needed could be in a table or list. A total 
should be listed as well as the individual items.  
 
RISK ANALYSIS  
Briefly describe the potential risks for the execution of the project. A risk is an uncertain event that, if 
occurs, has a positive or negative effect on project objectives. This will include how to lower or eliminate 
impact of the risk(s). Identify the r isks of not pursuing the project.  
 
SUMMARY PROJECT APPROVAL REQUIREMENTS  
Identifies who will sign the project charter and give authority to the project manager to carry out the 
project. Who decides/approves project milestones and signs off on the complete project. Identify a 
project team. Name and position title is included.
